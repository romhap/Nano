Can You Defer Your Place at an Italian Medical School After Being Accepted?

Rom Oct 11, 2026 · 4 min read

No - there's no formal deferral option for an IMAT place. Your offer is tied to that year's ranking list and intake only, so if you don't enroll within the window, it closes and the seat passes to the next candidate.

You went through the IMAT, made it onto a university's ranking list, and got your offer, and then life gets in the way: a family situation, a financial gap, a change of mind you want more time to be sure about. The question comes up more than you'd expect: can you just hold your place and start next year instead?

There's no formal deferral option

Unlike university systems where accepted students can request deferred entry and keep their offer waiting, Italian medical school admission through the IMAT doesn't work that way. Your offer is tied to that specific year's ranking list, for that specific intake. There's no mechanism to freeze a place and activate it twelve months later. If you don't enroll within the window the university sets, the offer doesn't carry over, it simply closes.

Why the ranking system doesn't bend this way

The IMAT places an entire cohort against a fixed number of seats using that year's exam and that year's applicant pool. Allowing deferrals would mean holding a seat out of circulation while a new ranking list gets generated the following year, which runs against the whole point of the system: filling every available seat from the current year's candidates, in order, as efficiently as possible. A seat that sits empty because someone deferred is a seat that should have gone to the next person waiting on it.

What actually happens if you decline

If you don't take the place this cycle, it goes back into the pool and moves down to the next candidate on the ranking list, the same way it would if you'd never been offered it at all. Your IMAT score from that sitting doesn't carry forward either, it belongs to that year's exam. Wanting to start later effectively means retaking the IMAT the following year and re-entering the whole process from the beginning, not picking up where you left off.

The real alternative: a leave of absence, after you've enrolled

If your actual goal is pausing rather than avoiding starting altogether, enroll first, then take the leave of absence your university's own policy allows once you're already a registered student. That's a completely different mechanism from deferring an offer: you keep your place in the program itself, instead of gambling it on a ranking list that resets every year. We've written separately about what that actually involves, and it's worth understanding before assuming deferring and pausing are the same option.

How to actually decide before you accept

Because there's no take-it-later option, the decision to accept has to be made with the information you have right now, not information you're hoping to have in a year. If you're genuinely unsure whether this is the right year to start, that's worth working through before results come out, not after, since by the time you're holding an offer, the two real choices left are enroll now or walk away and retake the exam later.

Trying to work out whether this is actually the right cycle for you to take your offer, or whether waiting and retaking makes more sense? That's exactly the kind of decision 1:1 mentorship helps you think through properly, before you're stuck choosing under pressure.
