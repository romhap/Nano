Should You Retake the IMAT or Look at a Different Country?

Rom Jul 2, 2026 · 4 min read

There's no universal answer: a retake tends to make sense when the gap to a seat was small and the cause was clearly fixable, while looking at a different country makes more sense when the gap was large or a previous retake already failed to move the needle.

This is one of the harder conversations we have with candidates, and it deserves a genuinely honest answer rather than a generic "never give up" one.

Start with an honest diagnosis, not a decision

Before deciding between retaking or looking elsewhere, get clear on why the first attempt didn't work out. Was it a specific, fixable weak section? A preparation timeline that was genuinely too short? A preference list that was too short or too concentrated on the most competitive names? Each of those points toward a different answer, and skipping this step tends to produce a decision based on emotion rather than an actual diagnosis.

When a retake genuinely makes sense

If the gap between your score and a realistic seat was small, or if the cause was clearly identifiable and fixable, a focused retake with a genuinely different approach, not just "more of the same," is often the more efficient path. A year is a real cost, but it's not automatically a wasted one if it's used deliberately.

When looking at a different country genuinely makes sense

If the gap was large, if your circumstances or timeline genuinely don't support another full prep cycle, or if you've already retaken once without meaningful improvement, it's worth being honest about whether a different route, a different country's system entirely, might be the more realistic path to the same underlying goal, becoming a doctor.

What we'd actually caution against

Deciding out of pure frustration in the immediate aftermath of results, in either direction. Give yourself a short amount of time to actually process the result before committing to a year-defining decision, then revisit it with a clearer head and an honest diagnosis of what happened.

Trying to actually diagnose what went wrong and which path makes sense for your specific situation? That honest, personal diagnosis is exactly what 1:1 mentorship is for, especially after a result that didn't go the way you hoped.

Apply for the mentorship

Neither path is automatically the right answer. The right answer depends on an honest read of what actually happened, not on which option feels less painful in the moment.
