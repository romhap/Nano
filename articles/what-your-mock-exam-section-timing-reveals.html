What Your Mock Exam Time-Per-Section Breakdown Actually Reveals

Rom Oct 7, 2026 · 4 min read

Most candidates finish a mock, look at the overall score, and move on. That single number is the least informative thing on the page. The far more useful data point is sitting right next to it, unread: how long you actually spent on each section, not just the total 100 minutes.

Why the overall time doesn't tell you much

Finishing with ten minutes to spare feels good, but it can mean two very different things. It can mean you moved efficiently through every section, or it can mean you rushed one section to bank time for another and never noticed. The total time, like the total score, averages away the exact information you need to actually fix something.

What to actually record

Note the clock, or a lap time if your mock platform gives you one, at the end of each section, not just at the end of the paper. You don't need a precise second-by-second log, just the rough minutes spent on General Knowledge, Logical Reasoning, Biology, Chemistry, and Physics & Maths individually. Put it next to your score for that section. That's the whole system.

The two patterns worth catching

The first is a section where your accuracy is fine but your time is consistently high, that's not a knowledge gap, it's a speed problem, and it needs timed drilling, not more content review. The second is the opposite: a section you finish fast with a weak score, which usually means you're answering with false confidence rather than actually knowing the material. Those two problems look identical on a plain score report and need completely different fixes.

What to do once you see the pattern

Track it across three or four mocks before you act, one mock's timing can just be an off day. If a section is slow every time, build dedicated timed reps for that section alone, shorter than full mocks, just drilling pace. If a section is fast and wrong every time, slow down specifically there on your next attempt and see whether your score actually improves. The breakdown tells you where to point the fix. The overall time never does.
