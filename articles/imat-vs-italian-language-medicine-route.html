IMAT vs the Italian-Language Medicine Route: Which Should You Choose?

Maya Jul 21, 2026 · 4 min read

If Italian isn't already close to fluent, the IMAT is almost certainly the more realistic near-term path; the Italian-language route only becomes a genuine option once you're already fluent.

Most candidates researching this find the IMAT first and assume it's the only door into Italian medical school. It isn't. There's a parallel, Italian-language track, admitted through a separate national test, and it's worth understanding both before you settle on a plan.

The core difference

The IMAT admits you into Medicine and Surgery, Dentistry, or Veterinary Medicine taught entirely in English. The Italian-language route admits students, through its own separate national test, into the same kinds of degrees taught in Italian. Same profession at the end, genuinely different journey to get there.

Why most international candidates default to the IMAT

The obvious reason: it doesn't require fluent Italian to sit the exam or follow the coursework, which makes it accessible to candidates who didn't grow up speaking the language. For a non-Italian-speaking candidate, the Italian-language route effectively requires near-native Italian proficiency well before the test even happens, which is a much steeper prerequisite for most international applicants.

Why some candidates still consider the Italian-language route

If you already speak fluent Italian, whether from family background, prior schooling, or years of study, it opens up a wider set of universities and seats than the English-taught track alone, since the Italian-language programmes run at more institutions across the country. It's a genuinely different calculation for someone starting from fluent Italian than for someone starting from zero.

How to actually decide

If Italian isn't already close to fluent, the IMAT is almost certainly the more realistic near-term path, since building exam-ready fluency in a language from scratch is a much bigger project than preparing for an English-language admissions test. If you're already fluent, it's worth genuinely comparing seat numbers and competition levels between the two routes rather than defaulting to whichever one you heard about first.
