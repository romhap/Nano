What Happens If You Fail an Exam in Italian Medical School

Rom Aug 31, 2026 · 4 min read

Getting a seat through the IMAT feels like clearing the hardest part. Then the first university exam session happens, someone fails a course everyone assumed was routine, and the fear that follows is usually way out of proportion to what actually happens next.

You almost certainly won't be kicked out

Italian universities don't run on a strict three-strikes system. Fail an exam and there's no permanent mark against you, no meeting with a dean, and no limit on how many times you can sit it again. Each course has multiple exam sessions a year, usually clustered around winter, summer, and autumn and known as appelli, and a failed attempt just means showing up for the next one better prepared. The system is built around eventually passing, not around punishing a bad attempt.

Where it actually costs you: time, not the degree itself

The real consequence isn't failure, it's falling behind schedule. Every degree has a set number of credits, known as CFU, attached to each year. Fall too far behind on collecting them and you become what's officially called fuori corso, meaning off the standard timeline. It doesn't end your enrollment, but it can mean an extra semester or year added onto six, and in some cases extra fees for the additional time.

The part that actually matters for your wallet

This is where fuori corso status has real teeth. Regional scholarships and subsidized housing, which a lot of students rely on, are usually conditional on hitting a minimum number of credits by a set date each year, not just on income. Fall behind that specific threshold and you can lose the benefit for the following year even while staying enrolled. The exact minimum varies by region and university, so it's worth checking your own school's rules directly rather than assuming it works the same everywhere.

What actually prevents this from becoming a pattern

Almost every student who ends up fuori corso got there the same way. One hard exam gets postponed past its first appello, a second one stacks on top while studying for the delayed one, and the backlog compounds from there. The habits that got you through IMAT prep, spacing out review instead of cramming, and tracking progress honestly instead of guessing, are exactly what keeps one rough exam from turning into a two-semester pileup.
