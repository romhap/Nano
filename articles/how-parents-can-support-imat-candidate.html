How Parents Can Actually Support an IMAT Candidate

Rom Jul 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Most of what we write is aimed directly at candidates. This one's for the people supporting them, because we get asked about this almost as often as we get asked about study strategy.

What genuinely helps

Consistent, low-pressure interest, asking how prep is going without immediately following up with "so what's your score right now," goes a long way. Practical support, helping manage logistics like registration deadlines or document gathering, takes real weight off a candidate's plate without adding to the emotional pressure of the exam itself.

What unintentionally adds pressure

Frequent score check-ins, even well-meaning ones, can start to feel like a running scoreboard rather than genuine support. Comparisons to siblings, family friends, or other candidates, even offhand ones, tend to land much harder than intended. And treating a single mock exam result as a serious verdict, rather than one data point in a long process, tends to amplify the stress rather than help manage it.

The financial and logistics conversation

This is genuinely useful to have early and honestly, covering roughly what studying and living in Italy will cost, what the application timeline actually looks like, and what happens under different outcomes, including a possible retake. Having this conversation calmly in advance is far less stressful than having it reactively under pressure later.

The single most useful thing

Trusting that your candidate is taking this seriously, and letting the day-to-day study decisions be theirs, tends to matter more than any specific piece of advice. This is already a high-stakes, high-pressure process from the inside. The most helpful role from the outside is usually steady, unconditional support, not additional oversight.

Want an outside, objective perspective on how your candidate's preparation is actually going? That kind of honest, third-party read is something 1:1 mentorship can offer that a parent, understandably, often can't.

Apply for the mentorship

This process is hard enough from the inside. The best thing a parent can usually offer is steady support, not another source of pressure.
