Why Your Practice Question Accuracy and Your Mock Exam Score Don't Match

Maya Sep 1, 2026 · 4 min read

You've been hitting 85-90% on untimed practice sets for weeks. Then you sit a full mock exam and come out well below that. It feels like proof that something's wrong, either the practice was fake progress or the mock was unfairly hard. Neither is true. The two numbers are measuring different things, and both are accurate.

Practice accuracy measures whether you know the material

Working through twenty biology questions in a row, with no clock and the option to pause and think, tests one thing: whether you understand the content. There's no fatigue built up from the two hours before it, no pressure from watching the time run down, and no cost from switching between chemistry logic and physics formulas within minutes of each other. High accuracy here is real and worth having. It just isn't the whole picture.

A full mock adds everything practice sets remove

The IMAT is a single sitting across multiple sections, and a full mock is the only format that reproduces that. By the time you reach the last section, you're running on the mental fatigue built up from everything before it. You're also paying a switching cost every time the question type changes, and a wrong early guess can quietly eat into the time you meant to spend later. None of that shows up in a set of isolated practice questions, and none of it is optional on the real day.

Both numbers are telling you something useful

A wide gap between the two isn't a red flag, it's a diagnosis. High practice accuracy with a much lower mock score usually means the content is solid but time management, pacing, or endurance across a full sitting needs work, which is a very different problem than not knowing the material. Track both numbers over time instead of trusting either one alone, and the gap itself becomes one of the more useful signals you have.

What actually closes the gap

It's not more untimed practice, since that only reinforces the skill you've already proven you have. It's more full-length, timed sittings that force you to build pacing and stamina under the same conditions as test day, then reviewing where the time actually went section by section. The gap tends to shrink fastest once fatigue and pacing get trained on their own, separately from content review.
