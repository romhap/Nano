The IMAT Application Costs Nobody Mentions Before Tuition

Maya Jun 29, 2026 · 4 min read

We've written separately about the bigger picture, tuition bands and living costs, but there's an earlier layer of expense that catches people off guard precisely because it's smaller and easy to overlook while focused on the bigger numbers.

Document translation and certification

Depending on your country and target university, transcripts and diplomas sometimes need official translation into Italian or English, plus certification of some kind. These costs vary a lot by country and provider, but they're rarely zero, and they're easy to forget about until a deadline is suddenly close.

Courier and postal costs

Some steps in the process still require physical documents to travel, whether to a consulate, a university, or back to you. International courier costs for time-sensitive documents add up faster than people expect, especially if something needs to be resent due to a formatting issue.

Travel for in-person steps

Depending on your situation, some steps, certain consulate appointments, for instance, may require an in-person visit rather than something handled remotely. If that means travel to a consulate in another city, that's a real cost worth budgeting for, not an afterthought.

The exam itself and any registration fees

Registration and exam-related fees are typically modest relative to the bigger costs ahead, but they're still a real, upfront cost that's worth confirming directly on Universitaly rather than assuming a number from a forum post, since fee structures can change between cycles.

How to actually budget for this stage

None of these individually are large. Together, over the course of an application cycle, they add up to a real, if modest, upfront cost separate from tuition and living expenses. Building in a buffer for this stage, rather than assuming the only real costs start once you're enrolled, avoids an unpleasant surprise mid-process.
