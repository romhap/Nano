Should Your Mock Exams Happen at the Same Time of Day as the Real IMAT?

Rom Nov 8, 2026 · 4 min read

Yes, at least for the mocks closest to test day. Most candidates schedule mocks whenever it's convenient, not at the time their actual IMAT slot starts, which means they've never actually tested how alert and focused they are at that specific hour. In the final stretch before the exam, that's a variable worth controlling instead of leaving to chance.

This isn't about superstition or needing everything to feel identical to test day. It's about one simple fact: how sharp you feel changes across the day, for almost everyone, and if you've only ever practiced at your favorite hour, you have no real data on how you perform at the hour that actually counts.

Why convenience quietly becomes the default

Early in prep, mocks get slotted in wherever they fit around school, work, or whatever else is happening that week. That's completely reasonable when the mock's job is to build content knowledge and pacing. But it means most candidates build a long track record of mock scores clustered around whatever time happens to suit their schedule, which may or may not be anywhere near when the real exam starts.

What actually changes across the day

Alertness, working memory, and the ability to sustain focus for a long stretch all fluctuate over the course of a day, and the pattern is different for different people. Some people are sharpest in the morning and flatten out by mid-afternoon. Others take a while to properly wake up and hit their best focus later. Neither is wrong, but if your mocks have only ever happened during your personal peak window, a mock score tells you what you can do at your best, not necessarily what you'll do at your actual slot's start time.

Where this actually bites people

The risk isn't that your score collapses on test day, it's smaller and sneakier than that: slightly slower reading in the first section, a few more re-reads than usual, a pacing plan that assumes a sharpness you don't quite have yet at that hour. None of that shows up as an obvious problem. It just quietly costs a handful of points you never see coming because you'd never tested yourself at that time before.

How to actually close the gap

You don't need to overhaul your whole mock schedule around this. Early and mid-prep mocks can stay wherever they fit, since their job is building content and pacing skill, not simulating test day. But in the final few weeks, deliberately book at least one or two full-length mocks to start at or close to your actual exam slot's time. Treat the result the same way you'd treat any other mock: check whether a specific section dragged, whether your pacing held up, and whether anything about that time of day surprised you.

If the mismatch is large, give yourself more runway

If your real slot starts noticeably earlier or later than when you normally study, don't wait until the final week to find out it matters. Start shifting a portion of your study sessions and at least a couple of mocks toward that time several weeks out, so your focus has time to adjust gradually instead of being asked to perform at an unfamiliar hour for the first time on the one day it actually counts.
