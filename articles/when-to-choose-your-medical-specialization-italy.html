After the IMAT

When You Actually Need to Choose a Medical Specialization in Italy

Rom Aug 28, 2026 · 4 min read

It comes up constantly, even from candidates who haven't sat the IMAT yet: "but what if I don't know what kind of doctor I want to be?" It's a fair question, and also one that assumes the decision is a lot closer than it actually is.

Why this worry shows up so early

Part of it is just how medical school gets talked about outside Italy, where some systems ask students to signal a direction earlier. Part of it is candidates projecting the IMAT's high stakes onto every decision that comes after it, as if one wrong guess now locks in the rest of your career. Neither assumption matches how the Italian system is actually structured.

What the six years actually decide

The Laurea in Medicina e Chirurgia is a general medical degree. Every student, regardless of what they think they might eventually want to do, goes through the same core curriculum and the same clinical rotations across the major specialties. Nothing about your specialization intentions changes your coursework or your exam list during these six years.

Where the real choice happens

Specialization is a separate, later step. After graduating and qualifying, you sit for entry into a scuola di specializzazione, essentially the Italian equivalent of residency, through a national ranking exam. That's the point where you actually declare a field and compete for a place in it, years after the IMAT, not during it.

What's actually worth doing now instead

Use the rotations you'll go through during the degree as real information gathering, not as a formality to get through. Most students walk into their clinical years with a vague guess and walk out with a genuinely different, better-informed one, because they'd actually seen the specialties up close rather than imagined them from the outside.
