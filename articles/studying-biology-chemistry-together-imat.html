How to Study IMAT Biology and Chemistry Together, Efficiently

Rom Jun 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Biology and Chemistry together make up close to two-thirds of the IMAT, and most study plans still treat them as two fully separate subjects to schedule in isolation from each other. That's a missed opportunity.

Where they genuinely overlap

Biochemistry, metabolism, and molecular biology topics sit at the actual intersection of both subjects, and studying them in isolation within just one subject's block often means revisiting the same underlying concepts twice, inefficiently, instead of once, thoroughly.

What interleaving actually looks like

Rather than a strict "Biology week, then Chemistry week" structure, deliberately schedule overlapping topics, enzyme kinetics, cellular respiration, organic molecules relevant to biological systems, close together in your calendar so the underlying chemistry reinforces the biology and vice versa.

Why this actually helps retention, not just efficiency

Seeing the same underlying concept from two angles, a biological process and its chemical mechanism, tends to build more durable understanding than memorizing each in isolation. It's genuinely easier to recall something you understand from two connected angles than something you memorized once in a single context.

How to actually build this into a schedule

You don't need to redesign your entire study plan around this. Identify the handful of genuinely overlapping topics between the two subjects and deliberately schedule them near each other, rather than letting your syllabus order dictate a strict separation that doesn't reflect how the content actually connects.
