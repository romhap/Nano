Dealing With Family Pressure During IMAT Prep

Rom Jun 14, 2026 · 4 min read

We wrote a piece aimed at parents about how to support a candidate well. This one is aimed the other way, at candidates dealing with family expectations that add real weight on top of an already high-stakes exam.

Naming the pressure helps more than people expect

A lot of family pressure is well-intentioned interest that lands as scrutiny. Simply naming that out loud, to yourself or to them, "I know you're asking because you care, and it's adding pressure I'm already carrying," tends to shift the dynamic more than silently absorbing it does.

Set boundaries around score check-ins, specifically

It's reasonable to ask family to check in about how you're doing generally rather than about specific mock scores after every practice session. A simple, direct request along those lines usually lands better than most candidates expect, and most families adjust once they understand why.

Separate their anxiety from your own

Family stress about the outcome is often about their own uncertainty, not a genuine judgment of your effort or ability. Recognizing that distinction, that their nervousness belongs to them and doesn't have to become yours too, is a real, learnable skill under this kind of pressure.

Keep the bigger picture conversation open

A calm, honest conversation about what happens under different outcomes, including a possible retake, tends to lower the temperature more than avoiding the topic entirely. Uncertainty left unspoken tends to generate more anxiety, for everyone, than a plan discussed openly in advance.

What we'd actually emphasize

You're allowed to want your family's support without needing their pressure. Those aren't the same thing, and it's fair to ask for one while gently pushing back on the other.

Want an outside perspective that isn't tangled up in the same family dynamic? That's exactly the kind of steady, objective support 1:1 mentorship can offer alongside your prep.

Family pressure is real, and it's fair to feel it. It's also fair to manage it deliberately rather than just absorbing it silently on top of everything else you're already carrying.
