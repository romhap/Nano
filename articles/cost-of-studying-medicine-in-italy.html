How Much Does It Actually Cost to Study Medicine in Italy?

Maya Jul 23, 2026 · 5 min read

Tuition at Italian public universities is income-based rather than flat, so the annual fee typically runs from a few hundred to several thousand euros depending on your family's declared income, and living costs on top of that vary enormously by city.

This is one of the most consistently underrated advantages of studying medicine in Italy, and also one of the most consistently misunderstood, because the real number depends on more than people expect.

Tuition is usually income-based, not flat

Most Italian public universities use an income-based fee structure rather than a flat tuition price. In practice, this means your actual annual fee can range from a few hundred euros to several thousand, depending on your family's declared income (via the ISEE system or an equivalent assessment for international families). This surprises a lot of candidates who assume "public university in Europe" automatically means one fixed cheap number. It's more accurate to say it's potentially very cheap, but you won't know your specific figure until you go through the assessment.

Living costs vary enormously by city

This is the part that actually moves the total budget the most. A room in a smaller university town can cost a fraction of what the same room costs in Milan or Rome. Food, transport, and general living expenses follow the same pattern. Two candidates at two different universities can have genuinely different total costs of living even with identical tuition bands, purely because of where they ended up.

The costs people forget to budget for

Beyond tuition and rent: health insurance (often required for your residency permit as a non-EU student), the residency permit process itself, textbooks, occasional exam re-sit fees if it comes to that, and the simple cost of flying home a couple of times a year if you're coming from far away. None of these are large individually, but they add up to a meaningful chunk that a "tuition plus rent" estimate misses.

How to actually estimate your own number

Don't rely on a single blog's total figure, including this one, because tuition bands and living costs both shift and both depend heavily on your personal circumstances. Use each university's own published fee calculator once you have a shortlist, and budget living costs based on the specific city, not a national average.

Trying to actually estimate a realistic total budget for your specific situation? That's a concrete, personal calculation, not a generic number, and it's exactly the kind of thing 1:1 mentorship helps you work through properly.
