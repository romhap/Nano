Is Your English Good Enough for the IMAT? Here's the Real Bar

Maya Aug 20, 2026 · 4 min read

A lot of candidates from non-English-speaking backgrounds worry about this before they've even looked at a single practice question: is my English actually good enough? It's a real concern worth taking seriously, but the bar is lower - and different - than most people assume.

You don't need native fluency

Every year, thousands of non-native English speakers sit and pass the IMAT. The exam is written for an international candidate pool by design - the science and maths questions use precise but not obscure vocabulary, and even the reading section (see our full breakdown of what it actually tests) is calibrated to be accessible, not a literature exam. If you can comfortably follow a university-level science article in English, you're already at or near the level the exam expects.

Where a real gap actually shows up

The honest risk isn't understanding individual words - it's reading speed under time pressure. If parsing an English sentence takes you noticeably longer than it would in your first language, that adds up across 60 questions in 100 minutes, especially in longer science question stems. That's a real, specific gap worth addressing directly, rather than a vague "my English isn't good enough" fear that's hard to actually act on.

How to close that specific gap

Read English-language science content regularly, not for grammar study but for speed and comfort - textbook chapters, science journalism, anything at the right level, consistently, so your brain stops translating and starts reading directly. And practice with real IMAT-format questions specifically, since getting comfortable with how this exam phrases questions matters more than general English study - the vocabulary and sentence patterns repeat far more than people expect once you've seen enough real papers.
