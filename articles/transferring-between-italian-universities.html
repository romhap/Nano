Can You Transfer Between Italian Universities After You've Started?

Rom Jun 12, 2026 · 4 min read

We get this question occasionally from candidates already enrolled somewhere who are wondering whether their initial choice is truly permanent. It's worth an honest, high-level answer, with the caveat upfront that the specifics are genuinely case-by-case.

The short answer

Transferring between Italian universities mid-degree is something that does happen and is possible in principle, but it's governed by each university's own admissions and transfer procedures, seat availability at the receiving university, and how your completed coursework is evaluated against theirs. It is not a simple, automatic process.

What tends to affect whether it's realistic

Available seats at your target university for the year you'd transfer, which isn't guaranteed to exist. How much of your completed coursework the receiving university is willing to formally recognize, which affects whether a transfer saves you time or effectively sets you back. And each university's own specific transfer policy, which genuinely varies and isn't something we can generalize accurately here.

Why we're not giving you specific steps

This is exactly the kind of process where the actual mechanics differ by university and by circumstance, and getting it wrong from a generic online answer, including this one, could cost you real time. The right first step is contacting the international student office at both your current and target universities directly and asking what a transfer would actually involve for your specific case.

What's worth thinking through before you even ask

Whether the underlying reason for wanting to transfer is something a transfer would actually fix, city fit, programme structure, versus something that would follow you anywhere, since a transfer is a real disruption worth being clear-eyed about before pursuing it.

Trying to think through whether transferring would actually solve the problem you're having? That honest, strategic reflection, not the administrative transfer process itself, is exactly what 1:1 mentorship can help you work through.
