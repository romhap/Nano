Biology

The Biology Topics That Show Up on the IMAT Every Single Year

Maya Aug 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Biology is 23 of the IMAT's 60 questions — more than a third of the entire exam, and nearly double the size of any other subject section. If you're only going to get ruthless about weighting one subject, this is the one.

The themes that keep recurring

Real papers, year after year, cluster around the same handful of core themes:

  • Cell biology — organelle structure and function, membrane transport, cell communication.
  • Molecular biology — DNA replication, transcription, translation, and how mutations disrupt each step.
  • Genetics and heredity — Mendelian ratios, inheritance patterns, allele frequency basics.
  • Bioenergetics and metabolism — glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, at the level of "what happens and why," not biochemistry-course depth.
  • Human anatomy and physiology — how the major organ systems actually function together.
  • Evolution and ecology — natural selection mechanics, basic ecological relationships.

Notice what's not on that list: obscure, research-level detail. The level tested is consistently mechanism-and-definition based — "what is X," "which process does Y" — not the kind of narrow specialist knowledge you'd only get from an advanced university course.

How to actually study this efficiently

Don't re-read a full biology textbook chapter by chapter — that's how weeks disappear without your score moving proportionally. Instead, keep a running list of the specific mechanisms you keep getting wrong on practice questions, and drill only those, repeatedly, until they're automatic. A shorter list, revisited often, beats a long syllabus skimmed once.

And cross-reference against real past questions as you go (see: I Read Every Past IMAT Paper So You Don't Have To) — it's the fastest way to tell which of these themes the exam actually leans on hardest in a given style, versus which ones show up only occasionally.
