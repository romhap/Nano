Should You Take a Gap Year Before the IMAT?

Rom Sep 7, 2026 · 4 min read

It depends: a gap year helps when it's used as a structured second prep cycle with a real plan behind it, but it doesn't help just because a year passes.

This question shows up constantly from two very different groups: people finishing high school who aren't sure they're ready yet, and people who already sat the IMAT once, didn't get the result they wanted, and are wondering whether an extra year changes anything. The honest answer depends less on the year itself and more on what you actually do with it.

Why this question comes up so often

The IMAT rewards depth in Biology and Chemistry that most school curricula don't fully cover by the time exam registration opens. Add Logical Reasoning and a General Knowledge section that draws from areas most students haven't studied deliberately, and it's easy to feel like one more year would close the gap. That instinct is usually correct. The part people skip is asking what specifically would be different by the end of it.

What a gap year actually buys you

Time, obviously, but more usefully: a second full run at the syllabus without exam pressure sitting on top of school deadlines, room to fix a genuinely weak section instead of patching it at the last minute, and a chance to sit mock exams under realistic conditions repeatedly rather than a handful of times squeezed into a busy final year. None of that happens automatically just because a year exists. It happens because you plan it like a second attempt, not a pause.

What it costs you

A year of your degree timeline, a year of tuition and living costs you haven't started earning against yet, and for some candidates, a year of explaining to family or friends why they're "still" preparing. None of these are small, and none of them are reasons to rule it out on their own. They're reasons to be specific about what the year is for before you commit to it.

The version that actually works

The gap years that pay off tend to look like a structured second prep cycle: a clear study schedule from week one, regular full-length mock exams from early on rather than only near the end, and an honest post-mortem of whatever went wrong the first time, if this isn't your first attempt. The ones that don't pay off tend to look like an extra twelve months of loosely intending to study more, with the same habits that didn't work before.

How to decide, honestly

Ask what would genuinely be different in month eleven compared to where you are now if you don't take the year. If the answer is "not much, because I haven't actually changed how I'm preparing," a gap year without a real plan behind it just delays the same outcome. If the answer is "I'd finally have covered Biology and Chemistry properly and sat a dozen realistic mocks," it's probably worth it.
