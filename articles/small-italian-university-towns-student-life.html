What Living in a Small Italian University Town Is Actually Like

Maya Jul 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Every prep forum talks about Milan, Rome, and a handful of other big names. Almost nobody talks about what it's actually like to study in one of the smaller university towns, which is a shame, because for a lot of candidates it's the better fit.

The upside people don't hear about

Lower cost of living, almost always. Rent, food, and general expenses in a smaller town go noticeably further than in a major city, which matters over six years, not just one semester. It's also easier to actually feel embedded in a place when the university is a bigger part of the town's identity, rather than one institution among thousands of things competing for attention.

The trade-off people don't hear about either

Fewer things to do outside of university life, a smaller international community in some cases, and less variety in day-to-day options, from food to nightlife to part-time work opportunities. For some candidates this is a non-issue. For others, especially those used to a lot of city stimulation, it's a genuine adjustment.

What it means for your studying, specifically

Fewer distractions can be a real advantage during an intense study period. It's genuinely easier to build a consistent routine when there's less pulling your attention in a smaller town, which matters more during IMAT prep and early exam-heavy semesters than people expect going in.

How to actually evaluate this for yourself

Don't rule a smaller town out just because you haven't heard of it, and don't rule the big cities out just because a forum said the smaller options are "sleepier." Be honest about what kind of environment you actually study and live well in, and weigh that against city size the same way you'd weigh any other factor on your list.
