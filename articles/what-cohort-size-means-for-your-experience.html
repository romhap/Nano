What Class Size Actually Means for Your Six Years

Rom Jun 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Seat numbers usually come up in one context: how competitive a university is to get into. Almost nobody talks about what that same number means once you're actually enrolled and living the degree day to day.

Smaller cohorts

A smaller intake tends to mean you genuinely know most of your classmates by name within the first year, closer relationships with faculty who see the same faces repeatedly, and a tighter, more intimate day-to-day environment. It can also mean fewer parallel elective options or student groups, simply because there are fewer people to form them.

Larger cohorts

A bigger intake usually means more anonymity in large lectures, at least early on, but also a wider social pool, more student organizations and interest groups, and sometimes more elective and specialization options later in the degree, since a larger cohort can support more variety.

Neither is objectively better

Some candidates thrive in a tight-knit smaller cohort and find a large one isolating despite the bigger social pool. Others prefer the anonymity and variety a larger cohort offers and find small cohorts claustrophobic. This is a genuine personality fit question, not a quality signal about the university itself.

How to actually factor it in

When you're comparing universities, look up approximate cohort size for each programme, not just overall seat competitiveness, and be honest with yourself about which environment you've actually thrived in before, in school or elsewhere, rather than assuming bigger or smaller is automatically the safer choice.
