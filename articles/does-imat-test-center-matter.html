Does Your IMAT Test Center Choice Actually Matter?

Rom Sep 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Every year we get a version of the same question in the weeks before registration closes: should I sit the IMAT in Italy, or somewhere closer to home? Candidates treat it like a strategic decision with a right and wrong answer, when in reality almost none of what actually decides your score depends on it.

The paper is identical everywhere, on the same day

The IMAT is held once a year, at the same time, with the same questions, at every participating test center worldwide. A candidate sitting it in Rome and a candidate sitting it in a test center abroad are answering the exact same paper under the exact same rules. There's no easier version, no local grading curve, and no home-turf advantage baked into the exam itself.

What genuinely does vary by location

Popular centers in major cities can fill their available seats faster, which occasionally pushes candidates toward a less convenient center once registration opens late. Room setup, chair comfort, and how strictly a given center enforces the small rules (phones, watches, what's on your desk) can differ slightly place to place, but none of it moves your score in a meaningful way. The exam format and time limit are fixed regardless of where you're sitting it.

The mistake candidates actually make

The mistake isn't picking the "wrong" center, it's spending real mental energy deliberating over a decision that has almost no bearing on the outcome, while the decisions that do matter, how you spend your final weeks of review, get less attention than they deserve. We see this most in candidates who delay registering because they're still weighing center options, and end up with less choice simply because they waited.

What's actually worth being deliberate about

Register early enough that your preferred center still has availability, factor in genuine travel logistics so exam-day morning isn't stressful, and confirm your specific center's exact requirements on Universitaly ahead of time so there are no surprises about what you can bring. Beyond that, the center is a logistics decision, not a strategic one, and it doesn't deserve the weight some candidates give it.

Spending more energy on logistics decisions than on your actual review plan for the weeks that matter? That's a common trap, and getting your priorities straight before test day is exactly what 1:1 mentorship helps you sort out.
