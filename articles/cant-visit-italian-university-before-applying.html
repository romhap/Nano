What to Do When You Can't Visit an Italian University Before Applying

Rom Sep 1, 2026 · 4 min read

Most people who rank Italian medical schools on their IMAT application have never set foot in the city, let alone the classroom. That's not a red flag on your application, it's just the normal situation for almost every international candidate. The problem isn't skipping the visit, it's not knowing what to check instead.

A campus tour wouldn't tell you much anyway

Even candidates who do fly out beforehand mostly come away with a feel for the buildings and the city, not the things that actually shape six years of study. Course structure, how early clinical exposure starts, and how a specific university runs its exam sessions don't show up on a walk around campus. You're choosing a system to move through, not a set of buildings to look at, and that system is fully documented whether or not you're standing in front of it.

What actually predicts your experience

Three things matter more than the campus itself: the teaching hospitals affiliated with the university and how much patient contact happens in the early years, the language track the degree runs in if that matters to you, and how the university structures its appelli, the exam sessions each course offers per year. All three are published in the university's own course guide or handbook, usually available as a PDF in English or easily translated, and they tell you far more about daily life than a photo of a lecture hall.

Where to get an honest read from a distance

Current students are the best source, and you don't need to fly anywhere to find them. Search the university's name alongside terms like "Erasmus" or "international student" on Reddit and TikTok, and look for people documenting an actual week rather than posting recruitment content. University subreddits and international student Facebook groups tend to surface the unglamorous details, cost of a room near campus, how crowded the anatomy labs get, whether professors reply to emails, that a promotional video never will.

The variable that outweighs all of it

Here's the part that catches people off guard: your IMAT score and how you rank your university preferences will do more to determine where you actually end up than any research you do about a specific campus. A candidate who obsesses over Padova versus Pavia but hasn't thought through how their likely score maps onto the national ranking is solving the wrong problem first.

Not sure how to rank your university choices when you can't visit any of them in person? Working through that strategy against your realistic score is exactly the kind of decision 1:1 mentorship helps with before submission day, not after.

Apply for the mentorship

Skipping the campus visit isn't the mistake it feels like. Skipping the handbook, the current-student research, and the honest look at where your score realistically places you is the one that actually costs people their first choice.
