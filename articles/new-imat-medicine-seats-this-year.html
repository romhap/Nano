New Medicine Seats Just Opened in Italy — Here's What That Means for You

Maya Aug 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Most lists of IMAT universities circulating online are working off last year's roster. That's a problem right now, because the roster itself just changed — and a brand-new option is exactly the kind of thing candidates either miss entirely or don't know how to think about when it comes to ranking.

Florence: a genuinely new programme

The University of Florence has launched its first fully English-taught Medicine and Surgery degree, hosted in the Careggi Medical District, with a single annual intake of 50 places open to both EU and non-EU applicants through the same nationally programmed entrance route as every other IMAT-accessible university. Because this is the inaugural cohort, there is no historical cutoff data for it at all — nobody can tell you "last year's score needed" because there wasn't a last year.

It's part of a broader pattern

Florence isn't an isolated case. The University of Cagliari, in Sardinia, is part of the same wider trend of the IMAT-accessible roster growing beyond the handful of names most candidates default to — Padova, Milano, Bologna, Roma, and a handful of others that dominate every prep forum. The list is bigger than most people researching it realize, and it keeps shifting cycle to cycle.

What "no cutoff data" actually means for your ranking

It cuts both ways, and it's worth being honest about that rather than pitching it as a guaranteed easy win. On one hand, a brand-new programme hasn't yet been discovered by the prep-forum crowd that piles onto the same well-known names every cycle — that can mean genuinely lighter competition in its first year or two, purely because fewer people know to rank it. On the other hand, first-year novelty can just as easily cut the other way: some candidates specifically seek out new programmes hoping for an easier entry, which can quietly push demand up instead of down. Nobody can promise you which way it goes before results are actually published.

What that means practically: don't rank a new programme purely as a "safe fallback" assuming it'll be easy — and don't skip it either just because you can't Google a cutoff. Weigh it the same way you'd weigh any option — location, course structure, seat count, how it fits your own priorities — and treat the missing historical data as a genuine unknown, not a shortcut in either direction.

Always check the current list yourself

Which universities are actually IMAT-accessible, how many seats each has, and which are new for a given cycle can all shift from year to year — always cross-check the current official list on Universitaly and each university's own admissions page before you finalize a ranking, rather than relying on any single article, including this one.
