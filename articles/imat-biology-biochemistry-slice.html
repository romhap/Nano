How Much Biochemistry Actually Shows Up in the IMAT Biology Section

Rom Oct 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Biochemistry and molecular biology make up a real but minority slice of the IMAT's Biology section - enough to know DNA replication, transcription and translation, and the big three metabolic pathways at a conceptual level, not enough to require full biochemistry-course depth.

Biochemistry has a reputation as its own dense university subject, so when candidates notice DNA replication or metabolism questions inside the IMAT's Biology section, a lot of them panic and reach for a full biochemistry textbook. Others go the opposite way and skip it entirely because "that's not real biology." Both reactions are wrong for the same reason: biochemistry and molecular biology are a real, recurring slice of the Biology section, just a much shallower one than either reaction assumes.

Where it actually sits inside Biology

The Biology section draws most of its questions from cell biology, genetics, human physiology, and general biological principles. Biochemistry and molecular biology questions, DNA and RNA processes, enzyme behavior, and core metabolic pathways, are layered on top of that as a recurring but minority slice, not a separate subject competing for equal study time. Treat it as one more topic inside Biology's existing footprint, not as an entire additional section to prepare from scratch.

What actually gets asked

Real papers test DNA replication, transcription, and translation at the level of knowing the steps, the enzymes involved, and what happens when one of those steps breaks down, not at the level of naming every intermediate in a multi-step pathway. The same goes for metabolism: you need to know what glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation each actually accomplish and roughly where they happen in the cell, not the full list of enzymes and intermediates a biochemistry course would require you to memorize.

The mistake candidates actually make

The failure mode isn't usually under-preparing this slice, it's over-preparing it. Candidates who already find biochemistry interesting tend to fall down a rabbit hole of pathway diagrams and enzyme names that the exam never rewards, time that would move the needle further if it went toward the physiology and genetics questions that make up a larger share of the same section. The other failure mode, ignoring it completely because it feels like "chemistry wearing a biology costume," leaves easy, recognition-level points on the table for no good reason.

How deep to actually go

Learn the DNA-to-protein pathway and the big three metabolic processes well enough to explain what each step does and why a disruption matters, then stop. That level reliably covers what actually appears on real papers. Anything past it, enzyme kinetics, detailed intermediate structures, biosynthetic side pathways, is studying for a biochemistry exam the IMAT doesn't give.

Not sure whether you're quietly over-studying one slice of Biology while under-studying the sections that carry more weight? That kind of misallocated study time is exactly what 1:1 mentorship catches early.

Apply for the mentorship

Biochemistry on the IMAT is real, recurring, and worth a deliberate slice of your Biology prep. It is not a second subject to master. Learn the core processes at the "what happens and why" level, resist the pull to go deeper than the paper ever asks, and that slice stops being a mystery and starts being a handful of reliable points.
