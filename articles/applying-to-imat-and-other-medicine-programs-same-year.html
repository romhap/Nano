Can You Apply to the IMAT and Other Medicine Programs in the Same Year?

Rom Sep 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Yes: there's nothing stopping you from applying to the IMAT while also pursuing other medicine programs, since registering for one doesn't disqualify you from the other - the real risk is a scheduling clash between offer deadlines, not eligibility.

Most candidates don't apply to the IMAT in isolation. They're also sitting the UCAT for UK schools, applying to a numerus clausus program elsewhere in the EU, or keeping a non-EU backup on the table. That's not indecision, it's sensible risk management given how competitive Italian medical school admission actually is. The real question isn't whether you're allowed to do this, you generally are, it's whether the timelines actually let you follow through on all of them without something quietly falling apart.

Why most candidates don't have to pick one

Registering for the IMAT doesn't commit you to Italy, and applying elsewhere doesn't disqualify you from the IMAT. There's no exclusivity clause built into the exam itself, and nothing about sitting it prevents you from continuing an application anywhere else in parallel. Most successful candidates we've worked with were juggling at least one other option right up until an actual offer forced a decision, not before.

Where the timelines can genuinely clash

The friction isn't in being allowed to apply everywhere, it's in the calendar. UK medical school offers through UCAS typically land months before IMAT results are published, which can mean you're asked to accept or decline a UK offer while you're still waiting to find out where the IMAT ranking actually puts you. Some other EU programs run on similarly early timelines. If you don't track these dates side by side in one place, you can end up deciding on one program while blind to where the other actually stands.

The mistake that actually catches people out

The real trap isn't the number of programs you're applying to, it's accepting a conditional offer or paying a deposit somewhere else without checking what that commitment actually requires. Some university offers come with a binding acceptance that closes off other options the moment you confirm it, even if you haven't heard back from Italy yet. Read the fine print on any offer before you accept it, specifically whether accepting locks you out of pursuing anything else, rather than assuming you can quietly walk it back later.

How to run it without dropping a thread

Keep a single timeline with every relevant deadline from every program you're actually pursuing, not just the IMAT's. Note which offers are binding the moment you accept and which ones give you a grace period. When two deadlines genuinely collide, work backward from what you'd actually do in each outcome rather than defaulting to whichever one is closer. Running parallel applications is normal. Losing track of which one just became binding is the actual risk.

Juggling the IMAT alongside other programs and not sure how the deadlines actually line up? Getting the sequencing right so you don't accidentally lock yourself out of an option is exactly the kind of planning 1:1 mentorship helps you get ahead of.
