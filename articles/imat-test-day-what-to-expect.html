What Actually Happens on IMAT Test Day

Rom Aug 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Most of what candidates prepare for is the content. Almost nobody mentally rehearses the day itself - and the day itself has its own logistics and its own particular kind of pressure that catches well-prepared people off guard more often than you'd expect.

The logistics, in general terms

You'll need valid photo ID and your registration confirmation - check your specific test centre's exact requirements on Universitaly well before the day, since these can vary and it's genuinely not worth risking a mistake here. Arrive with real margin, not exactly on time - test centres run identity checks and seating before the clock starts, and a stressful last-minute arrival is the worst possible way to walk into 100 minutes of concentration.

Bring only what you're told you're allowed to bring. Paper exams typically mean pen, ID, and little else at your desk - no notes, no phone within reach. Confirm the specifics for your centre ahead of time so this is a non-issue on the day itself.

The part nobody prepares for

The hour before the exam is where most of the damage happens, and it's rarely about content. It's the sudden awareness that this specific sitting decides a lot, surrounded by hundreds of other visibly nervous candidates, with nothing left to actually study. That combination creates a spike in anxiety that has nothing to do with how prepared you actually are - and if you've never experienced it before, it can feel like evidence you're not ready, when it's really just the room.

How to actually handle it

Simulating full timed mock exams beforehand (see: doing mock exams properly) means the 100-minute format itself isn't a surprise - your nervous system has already been through the shape of it. For the waiting-room anxiety specifically, having a fixed, boring routine for that last hour - the same breakfast, the same arrival time, the same few minutes of quiet before you go in - gives your brain something familiar to hold onto instead of spiraling on what's about to happen.

And once the clock starts: work your strongest section first if the format allows it, don't let one hard question eat time meant for easier ones later, and trust the elimination-based guessing math over blind panic on anything you're unsure of.
