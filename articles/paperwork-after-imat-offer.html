After the IMAT

The Paperwork You Actually Need After You Get Your IMAT Offer

Maya Jul 1, 2026 · 5 min read

Getting an offer feels like the finish line, and emotionally it is a huge one. Practically, there's still a real checklist between the offer and your first day of class, and it's worth knowing what's on it.

Formally accepting your seat

Offers typically come with a specific acceptance window, and missing it can mean losing the seat to the next candidate in the ranking. This is usually the first, most time-sensitive step, and it's worth confirming the exact deadline the moment you receive an offer, not assuming it matches what you read somewhere online.

Enrollment paperwork with the specific university

Separate from whatever you submitted to register for the IMAT itself, most universities require their own enrollment paperwork once you've accepted a seat, often including some of the same documents covered in our starting checklist, your diploma, transcripts, and for non-EU students, your Declaration of Value or CIMEA statement.

For non-EU students: the residency permit process

Once you're actually planning to move, non-EU students typically need to apply for a study visa before arrival and then convert it into a residency permit (permesso di soggiorno) shortly after arriving in Italy. This process has its own deadlines and its own local bureaucracy, generally handled through the local questura, and it's worth starting as early as your visa allows rather than leaving it until the semester has already begun.

Housing, which moves faster than people expect

Good student housing near your specific university can fill up well before term starts, especially in the more popular cities. It's worth starting this search the moment you've accepted your seat, not waiting until the enrollment paperwork is fully finished.

The reminder that applies here too

Exact deadlines and required documents shift by university and by year, so treat this as a checklist of what to expect, not a substitute for your specific university's own enrollment instructions once they're issued to you directly.

The paperwork is real, but it's not where most candidates actually need the most help. Once you're enrolled, having a mentor to help you hit the ground running academically, not with visas or documents, is what 1:1 mentorship is for.

Apply for the mentorship

The offer is the hard part in terms of competition. What comes after is mostly a matter of staying organized, not a second exam to worry about.
