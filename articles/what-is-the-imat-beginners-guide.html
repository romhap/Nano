What Is the IMAT, Actually? A Plain-English Breakdown

Maya Jul 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Before anyone starts prepping seriously, they usually spend a few confused weeks Googling acronyms. So let's start from zero, no jargon.

What the IMAT actually is

The International Medical Admissions Test (IMAT) is the entrance exam that Italian public universities use to admit students into their English-taught Medicine and Surgery, Dentistry, and Veterinary Medicine degrees. It's a single national test, sat once a year, the same day, at the same time, at every participating university and at a number of foreign test centres. Your score, combined with which universities you list as preferences, determines whether you get offered a seat, and where.

Who actually sits it

Anyone, EU or non-EU, who wants to study medicine, dentistry, or veterinary medicine in English at an Italian public university. You don't need an Italian background, an Italian passport, or prior study in Italy. What you do need is to register through the correct channel for your citizenship status, since EU and non-EU applicants follow slightly different tracks before the exam itself.

What's actually on it

As of the current format, it's 60 multiple-choice questions in 100 minutes, split across five sections: Logical Reasoning, General Knowledge, Biology, Chemistry, and Physics & Maths. Biology carries the most weight by a wide margin, Chemistry is second, and the other three sections are smaller but not negligible. We've written a full breakdown of the syllabus and each section separately, since one paragraph here can't do it justice.

How the score actually turns into a seat

After the test, everyone gets ranked nationally by score. You list universities in order of preference beforehand, and the system works through that ranking, offering seats at your highest-preference university where your score and the available places allow it. It's a centralized, structured process, not something each university runs independently, which is part of why understanding the ranking mechanics matters as much as the studying itself.
