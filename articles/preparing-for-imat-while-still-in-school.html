How to Actually Prepare for the IMAT While You're Still in School Full-Time

Maya Sep 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Almost every IMAT study plan you find online quietly assumes the same thing: that you have a mostly empty day to fill with prep. For a lot of candidates, that's not true. You're still in school, still doing homework, still sitting your own end-of-year exams, and IMAT prep has to fit into whatever hours are actually left over, not the other way around.

The time budget problem nobody says out loud

If school and homework already take up nine or ten hours of a weekday, a plan built around three hours of daily IMAT study simply doesn't exist for you on paper, and trying to force it usually just produces guilt instead of progress. The fix isn't finding more hours that aren't there. It's being honest about how few you actually have, then making sure every one of them is spent on something that moves your score, not just something that feels productive.

What has to give, on purpose

Something has to be smaller during this stretch, and it's better to choose it deliberately than to have it happen by accident. That might mean fewer hours on hobbies or a part-time job for a few months, or it might mean accepting a slightly lower bar on school subjects that don't overlap with the IMAT syllabus at all. The candidates who burn out fastest are usually the ones trying to keep every commitment at full intensity while adding IMAT prep on top, rather than anywhere.

Let school do double duty where it actually can

Biology, chemistry, and physics classes at school aren't wasted time next to IMAT prep, they're often covering the same underlying material from a different angle. Instead of treating schoolwork and IMAT prep as two separate piles, look for the overlap and let one pass do both jobs: revising a school chemistry topic with an eye toward how the IMAT tests it is more efficient than studying it twice, once for a school test and once for the exam.

A weekly shape that survives an actual school week

Short, fixed sessions beat vague intentions to "study more when there's time," because that time rarely shows up on its own. Thirty to forty-five focused minutes on weekday evenings, then longer blocks on weekends when school pressure eases up, adds up over months even though no single session looks impressive. Protect the weekend blocks especially, since they're usually where the real depth happens, past questions, timed sections, the things that don't fit into a rushed weekday half hour.

Not sure how to actually split limited hours between school and the IMAT syllabus? That's exactly the kind of tradeoff mentorship helps sort out, with a plan built around the time you really have.

Apply for the mentorship

You don't need a completely free schedule to prepare seriously for the IMAT. You need a realistic one, built around the hours that genuinely exist, used deliberately instead of hopefully.
