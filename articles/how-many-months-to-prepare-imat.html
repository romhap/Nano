How Many Months You Actually Need to Prepare for the IMAT

Maya Aug 2, 2026 · 4 min read

"How many months do I need?" is the question I get asked more than any other. It also doesn't have one honest answer — anyone who gives you a fixed number without asking about your starting point is guessing, not advising.

Why the generic answer is useless

A student who just finished a strong high school science track is starting somewhere completely different from someone who hasn't touched chemistry in three years. Treating both as needing "two months intensive" or "a full year" ignores the one variable that actually matters: how far you currently are from exam-ready, not how far anyone else is.

Before you pick a number, get an honest read on where you actually stand. That's a five-minute problem to solve, not a guessing game.

A structure that works regardless of your timeline

Whether you end up with three months or nine, the shape of good prep barely changes — only how much time each phase gets:

  1. Foundation. Close the biggest content gaps first, weighted by section — biology and chemistry first, since together they're 38 of 60 questions.
  2. Targeted practice. Real past-paper questions, by topic, with immediate review of why each wrong answer was wrong — not passive re-reading.
  3. Timed simulation. Full mock exams under the real 100-minute clock, close enough to test day that the format itself no longer feels unfamiliar.

A longer timeline gives phase one more room to breathe. A shorter one compresses it — but phase three, timed simulation, should never be the part that gets cut. Running out of time to simulate the real clock is a far more common failure point than running out of time to learn content.

The trap on the other end

It's not just candidates who start late who struggle. Starting a full year out with no real structure often leads to the opposite problem: burnout long before test day, because "I have plenty of time" quietly turns into inconsistent, directionless studying with no urgency behind it. More months isn't automatically better — more focused months is.
