How to Build an IMAT Study Schedule From Scratch

Rom Jul 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Most "study schedule" content online is really just someone else's finished schedule, which is far less useful than the process for actually building your own. Here's that process.

Step one: get an honest baseline

Before building anything, take a full-length timed mock exam cold, no prep, just to see where you're actually starting from section by section. This isn't about the score, it's about identifying which sections need the most work relative to each other.

Step two: work backward from test day

Count the weeks you actually have, then work backward, not forward, from exam day. Leave the final few weeks for full mock exams and review, not new content, since cramming new material right up to the exam consistently performs worse than consolidating what you already know.

Step three: allocate time by weight, then adjust for your baseline

Start from the actual section weighting, Biology and Chemistry get the most hours, then adjust based on your own baseline results. If you're already strong in a heavily-weighted section, you can trim its hours somewhat in favor of a genuine weak point, even a smaller one.

Step four: build in review cycles, not just forward progress

A schedule that only moves forward through new content, without scheduled return visits to earlier material, quietly loses ground to forgetting. Build in weekly or biweekly review blocks that revisit earlier topics, not just this week's material.

Step five: treat the schedule as a draft, not a contract

Revisit it every few weeks against your actual mock exam results. A schedule that isn't updated as your weak points shift is just a piece of paper, not an actual plan.
