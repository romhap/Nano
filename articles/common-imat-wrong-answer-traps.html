The Most Common Wrong-Answer Traps on the IMAT

Maya Jun 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Distractors on a well-built exam aren't random noise. They're built around the specific mistakes real candidates actually make, which means recognizing the pattern is its own kind of preparation.

The near-miss number

In calculation-heavy sections, a common trap answer is what you'd get from one specific, plausible slip, a sign error, a misplaced decimal, using the wrong formula variant. These options are deliberately close to the correct one, which is exactly why rushing the final step of a calculation is so costly.

The half-right statement

In reasoning and reading questions, a common trap is a statement that's true in isolation but doesn't actually answer what was asked, or that contradicts a specific detail in the passage while sounding generally plausible. These reward careful, literal reading over confident skimming.

The overly broad or overly narrow option

Especially in biology and reading questions, one common trap is an answer that overgeneralizes a specific fact, or narrows a general principle too far. Both sound reasonable if you're not checking the exact scope of what's being claimed against the exact scope of the question.

The outdated or commonly confused fact

Some traps rely on a widely believed misconception rather than a calculation error, testing whether you actually know the correct version of a commonly garbled fact, not just a related topic.

How to actually train against these

When reviewing practice questions you got wrong, don't just note the correct answer, identify which category of trap caught you. Over time this builds a genuine instinct for slowing down at exactly the moment a trap is most likely to appear, rather than a vague sense of "I need to be more careful."
