Nobody Explains the EU vs Non-EU IMAT Process Properly - So Here It Is

Maya Aug 4, 2026 · 5 min read

I get asked this constantly, and most explanations I've seen online are either outdated or written for one specific situation and presented as universal. So here's the version I wish someone had given me straight: the exam is identical for everyone. The process around it is not, and confusing the two costs people real time.

Same exam, different track

Every candidate - EU, non-EU, Italian resident or not - sits the exact same paper: 60 questions, same format, same scoring. What differs is which track your application sits on, and each track has its own seat pool, its own cutoff, and critically, its own timeline of what needs to happen and by when.

The EU / Italian-resident track

If you're an EU citizen or already legally resident in Italy, your process is comparatively straightforward: register for the exam directly through Universitaly, sit it, rank your university preferences, and the placement algorithm does the rest. The exam itself is effectively the only major hurdle in your path.

The non-EU track

If you need a student visa to study in Italy, there's a second process running in parallel to exam prep, and it is not optional. Non-EU pre-enrolment typically goes through your local Italian consulate or embassy and involves credential recognition (commonly via CIMEA) before you're even eligible to sit the exam on that track. This process consistently runs on an earlier timeline than most candidates expect - it needs to start well before the exam itself, not after you get your score.

This is the single most common planning mistake I see from non-EU candidates: treating the exam as the first deadline, when the visa and pre-enrolment paperwork is often the one that actually comes first.

What to actually do about it

Figure out which track you're on before anything else - before you buy a book, before you build a study plan. It determines your real deadlines, not just your seat pool. If you're non-EU, contact your local Italian consulate early and confirm exactly what CIMEA credential recognition and pre-enrolment require for your specific country, since requirements do vary. And always treat universitaly.it and your consulate's own page as the final word - dates and requirements shift, and a wrong assumption here can cost you an entire admissions cycle, not just points on the exam.

Once you know which track you're actually on, the real question is how to build your exam prep and university list around that real timeline. That's exactly the kind of strategic planning 1:1 mentorship helps you get right - not the consulate paperwork itself, but making sure your prep and university choices actually fit the deadlines you're working with.

Apply for the mentorship

The exam rewards preparation. The process around it rewards knowing which deadlines are actually yours. Don't let the second one undo the work you put into the first.
