After the IMAT

The Licensing Exam Nobody Mentions Until Your Final Year of Italian Medical School

Maya Sep 10, 2026 · 4 min read

Every stage of Italian medical school gets talked about eventually, the first semester, the clinical years, choosing a specialization. What almost nobody mentions early on is that finishing your degree and being licensed to actually practice medicine aren't the same milestone. There's a state exam sitting between them, and most candidates only hear about it in detail once they're already in their final year.

Graduating isn't the same as being licensed

Passing your last university exam gets you a degree. Being allowed to practice as a doctor requires a separate state licensing step, the Esame di Stato, which historically involved a further round of assessment after graduation. In recent years this has increasingly been folded into the final year of the degree itself for many universities, through supervised clinical practice and coursework that doubles as the licensing requirement. The exact setup has shifted more than once and varies by university, so it's worth checking your specific programme's current arrangement rather than assuming it works the way it did for someone who graduated a few years ago.

What it's actually assessing

Regardless of the exact format your cohort ends up with, the underlying goal is the same: proving you can function as a doctor in practice, not just recall material in an exam hall. That typically means demonstrated clinical competence during supervised placements, evaluated by the doctors you're actually working under, rather than a single written test you can cram for the week before.

Why it's worth knowing years ahead of time

Because this step is baked into how the final year or two of the degree is structured, showing up and doing the minimum during your clinical placements has consequences beyond your coursework grade. It's a different kind of pressure than exam prep, spread out over months instead of concentrated into a single testing day, and it rewards consistent competence far more than a strong final push. Candidates who treat their placements purely as a formality tend to find that attitude catches up with them here.

What to actually do about it now

If you're still years away from your final rotations, there's nothing urgent to prepare. The useful move is just knowing this step exists, so that when your university explains its version of the process, you're not hearing about it for the first time in a panic. Ask upperclassmen at your specific university how it worked for their cohort, since the details genuinely differ by school and by year.
