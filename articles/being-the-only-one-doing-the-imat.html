What It's Like Being the Only One You Know Taking the IMAT

Maya Sep 29, 2026 · 4 min read

Everyone else in your year is deep in UCAS personal statements, or US premed GPA math, or a completely different application cycle with its own deadlines and vocabulary, and you're the one person quietly working through IMAT past papers with nobody nearby who has ever sat this exam. That specific flavor of isolation is real, and it's different from ordinary exam stress, because it's not about the material being hard. It's about having no one close enough to check your reality against.

Why this exam is uniquely hard to talk about

Most standardized admissions tests have a local peer group built in, a school cohort all sitting the same UCAT sitting, a whole grade applying to the same handful of systems. The IMAT doesn't work that way for most candidates. It pulls people from dozens of countries into one exam that almost nobody in your daily life has heard of, let alone sat themselves. Friends going through familiar processes can offer generic encouragement, but they can't tell you whether your practice score is actually on track, because they have no reference point for what "on track" even means here.

What the isolation actually costs you

It's rarely the studying itself. Plenty of candidates are perfectly capable of working through material alone. What's missing is the everyday reality check other applicants take for granted, someone nearby who can glance at a score and say "that's normal" or "that's actually a problem." Without it, ordinary doubt has nothing to correct it, so a single bad section on a mock exam can spiral into a much bigger story than it deserves, purely because there's no one around to talk you back down.

Where the other IMAT candidates actually are

They exist, just not next to you. They're spread across time zones, posting practice breakdowns, comparing notes in prep forums and group chats, sitting the same registration deadlines you are. The isolation is geographic, not numerical. Once you go looking for that scattered group deliberately, instead of waiting for it to show up in your own city, the "nobody else is doing this" feeling usually shrinks fast.

What actually helps this specific kind of lonely

Not a full study group, that trades one problem for another. What helps is finding one or two other candidates further along in the same process, people who've already seen an IMAT sitting and know roughly what a given score means, so you have somewhere to point your doubt besides your own head. An outside, informed read on where you actually stand does more for this feeling than any amount of solo reassurance.
