Logic

The IMAT Section Most People Completely Ignore — And Shouldn't

Rom Aug 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Logical Reasoning & Problems is only 5 of the IMAT's 60 questions — the smallest section by far. Which is exactly why almost nobody dedicates real practice time to it, and exactly why it might be the single highest-ROI section to actually train on purpose.

Why it gets ignored

Biology, chemistry, and physics all have a syllabus — a defined content list you can point to and say "I need to learn this." Logic doesn't work that way. There's no chapter to read, no formula sheet to memorize, so most candidates never schedule time for it at all. It just becomes "whatever's left over," which in practice usually means zero deliberate preparation until test day itself.

What's actually being tested

Real papers show short, self-contained puzzles: propositional and deductive reasoning ("if X then Y; not Y; therefore..."), simple probability and percentages, basic combinatorics, spatial or seating-arrangement puzzles. Each one is solvable in under a minute if you've seen the pattern before — and each one can eat five minutes of confused re-reading if you haven't.

Why that makes it high-ROI

Because logic questions aren't gated behind content knowledge, they're one of the fastest sections to train — you're not relearning biology, you're pattern-matching a small number of puzzle types until you recognize them instantly. A handful of focused practice sessions can take you from "this always trips me up" to "this is free points" faster than the same hours would move your biology or chemistry score.

And because almost nobody else bothers to train it deliberately, the few candidates who do gain an edge that's disproportionate to the five questions on paper — both in points earned and in time saved that would otherwise go to confused re-reading mid-exam.
