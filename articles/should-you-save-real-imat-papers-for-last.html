Should You Save Real Past IMAT Papers for Your Final Mocks?

Maya Oct 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Unofficial mock questions are effectively unlimited. There's always another practice set, another question bank, another timed section you can print out tomorrow. Real retired IMAT papers aren't like that. There's a fixed, fairly small number of them, and once you've sat one under real timing, it stops being able to tell you anything new. That makes a decision most candidates never actually make on purpose: when in your prep timeline you spend each one.

Why real papers don't behave like unofficial mocks

A well-made unofficial mock can target the right syllabus topics, but it can't fully reproduce the exact pacing, phrasing, and density of traps the real exam uses, because it wasn't written by the same body under the same constraints. A retired official paper is the only resource that does this exactly, which is also exactly why it's limited. There's no way to generate more of them, only to find the ones that already exist.

What burning them early actually costs you

Using a real paper in the first month of prep, before you've built up section-specific weaknesses or settled into a pacing strategy, doesn't give you an accurate signal. At that stage almost everything feels hard, so the score tells you what you already knew, that you're early in the process. Worse, you've now spent one of a small number of resources that was uniquely suited to answer a question you weren't ready to ask yet: where would I actually land under real conditions, right now.

What saving them protects

Held back for the final few weeks, a real paper does something no unofficial mock can fully substitute for. It gives you a true readout of where you'd place under the exact conditions you'll face, at the point in your prep where that readout actually matters, close enough to test day that you can still act on what it shows you, but late enough that the result reflects your real level rather than your starting point.

Where unofficial mocks still earn their place

None of this makes unofficial mocks less useful, it just means they're suited for a different job. The bulk of the middle months, repeated section practice, drilling a specific weak topic, building timing habits, is exactly what they're for, and using up real papers on that kind of repetition would be a waste either way. A reasonable split is one real paper early as a rough baseline, and the rest saved for calibration checkpoints toward the end, rather than scattered evenly across the whole timeline.
