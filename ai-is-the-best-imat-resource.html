The Best IMAT Resource Isn't a Book or a YouTube Video

Rom 6 min read

Short answer: a strong AI model beats every static resource you'll find for IMAT prep, because it's the only one that can follow your exact confusion back to its root and fix it on the spot. The one we'd point you to right now is Claude Opus.

I studied for the IMAT completely alone, no course, no tutor. The slowest part was never content, it was getting stuck on something a book couldn't explain any differently than it already had, and a YouTube video couldn't explain at all because it wasn't made for my specific confusion. If the tools available today had existed back then, I'd have used them from day one.

Why books max out fast

A good textbook is necessary. It gives you content depth, the mechanisms and definitions the exam actually draws from. But a book is fixed. It explains a concept exactly one way, in exactly one order, and if that explanation doesn't click for you, the book has nothing else to offer. You either re-read the same paragraph hoping it lands differently the second time, or you move on half-understanding it. Neither is actually learning.

Why YouTube is worse, not better

Video feels more engaging than a page of text, which is exactly the trap. A video was made for a general audience, not for the specific gap in your understanding, so you end up watching ten minutes of content to get the thirty seconds that's actually relevant to you, and the explanation still isn't tailored to where you got stuck. It's passive by design. You watch, you don't get tested, and watching someone else solve a problem correctly tells you almost nothing about whether you could solve the next one yourself.

What an AI model actually gives you that neither can

The difference is that it can respond to you specifically. Paste in the exact question you got wrong and it can trace the error back to the one concept you misapplied, not re-explain the whole topic from scratch. Ask it to quiz you on just that weak point and it will, immediately, with as many follow-up questions as you need until it's actually solid. It doesn't get tired of explaining the same thing five different ways until one of them clicks, and it's available at 1am the night before a mock exam when no tutor is.

Why Claude Opus specifically

Not every AI model is built for this. The kind of problems the IMAT tests, multi-step chemistry, physics reasoning, logic puzzles with a trick buried in the wording, need a model that actually reasons through the steps carefully instead of pattern-matching to a surface-level answer. Opus is Anthropic's most capable model for exactly that kind of careful, multi-step reasoning, which is why it holds up on IMAT-style questions in a way lighter, faster models often don't. It's also simply better at explaining itself: showing its work, not just the final answer, which is the whole point when the thing you're trying to learn is the reasoning, not just the result.

One honest caveat: no AI model is infallible, and you should never take an explanation as gospel without checking it against a real source when something feels off. Use it to think through problems and explain your mistakes, not as a replacement for actually knowing the real syllabus.

How to actually use it

  • When you get a practice question wrong, paste the exact question and your reasoning, and ask it to find the specific step where your logic broke, not just the right answer.
  • Pick your single weakest subtopic and ask it to generate practice questions only on that, at IMAT difficulty, until you stop missing them.
  • After a mock exam, feed it your wrong answers as a batch and ask it to group them by root cause. Most candidates aren't weak at a whole section, they're weak at two or three specific concepts repeating across a section.
  • Ask it to explain a concept a second way if the first explanation didn't land. That's the one thing a book can never do for you.

An AI model is a tool, not a strategy. It can explain any concept you throw at it, but it won't tell you which concepts are actually worth your time this week. That's what mentorship is for.

Apply for the mentorship

Buy the book if you need the content depth. Skip the hour of YouTube. But for the part where you're actually stuck, explaining your specific confusion to a model that can reason through it with you will get you unstuck faster than anything else available right now.
